Chapter Seven — The After

Nothing collapsed.

Whoever tells you that a valley which has buried five of its own in one grave rises against its masters has read it in a book written far from any pit. The Vesta turned coal the Monday after the verdict and every Monday after that. Rent was held from wages. The store's book opened and closed on the week like a mouth. The cage ran, the screens shook, the rattle lived under the town like a floor. To any rider passing on the moor road that winter — to the Company's board, reading its returns in the city — the valley was what it had always been.

The returns were even good. For a while.

What had changed did not report to anyone, because what had changed was the size of a half-second, and no instrument the Company owned could weigh it.

Marek saw it first at the cage-side, a week after the pit resumed: the banksman with his hand on the gate, receiving the underlooker's order to load men before the second inspection had come up — a thing done a hundred times a year, always, everywhere, the ordinary trimming of the rules that kept the machine to its schedule — and the banksman did not refuse. Nothing so large. He paused. Half a breath, one look, level, at the underlooker's face — a look that checked something — and then he said, "As you order it, Master Ansel," and did it. As you order it. Four words, where for twenty years the order had needed none at all, had been simply absorbed into the doing as if the doing were nature. The underlooker felt it — Marek watched him feel it — a small weight arriving in his hands that had always ridden elsewhere, and he was a plain man and could not have named what he had been handed, and he carried it badly all that shift.

Within the month the four words were everywhere, in twenty forms. As you order it. If you'll mark that in the book, I'll go to it. Just so I have it right — it's the third pillar you're wanting worked? Perfectly civil, every syllable. Procedurally spotless — the men had never in their lives been so exact about procedure; the bond, the rules, the daybook were suddenly honored the way scripture is honored by a congregation that has stopped believing and started reading. The deputies' daybook, that masterpiece of the softened word, went hard overnight: the east roof is working at the second pillar and wants timber, wrote the surviving deputy now, morning after morning, in a hand that pressed through the page, and dared the office to teach him again the dialect it had wept over at the inquest. Requisitions came up signed by whole crews. Orders came down and were asked back — confirmed — recorded. Accountability, which had flowed downhill through the chain for a hundred years, light as grammar, was being handed back up it link by link, a gram at a time, in writing, with courtesy.

The chain had never been built for reverse flow. Watching Ansel receive a respectful request to confirm his own instruction in the book, Marek understood he was seeing a thing without a name yet: the machine feeling friction from its own rules. The forms, it turned out, could be held in either direction. It had simply never occurred to anyone below to grip them.

And the margin was withdrawn.

This was the deep change, the one that took even Marek — who kept accounts by nature — a full season to see whole, because it was made of things stopping, and a thing stopping makes no entry.

The pit had run, all his life, on a currency that appeared in no ledger: the free margin its people gave it. The greaser topping a dry axle he passed, unasked. The hewer trimming his own count fair when the tub ran over, throwing the Company the benefit. The putters straightening a rail on their own dinner-hour because a straight rail was a kindness to the next lad. The thousand daily judgment calls made quietly in the Company's favor, at every junction of the works, by men who had never once been paid for judgment — the sixpence of extra care in every shilling of wage, donated, forever, because that was what work was, because a man's craft was his own even where nothing else was.

It stopped. Not by meeting, not by oath — the valley had learned what meetings earned, and took no oath, and could not have been convicted, any of them, of a single act. It stopped the way frost comes, everywhere on the same night, out of a shared air. Men did what the bond said, whole and exact, and nothing beside. The dry axle squealed until the man whose book it was in greased it. The over-full tub went to the beam over-full and was laid out, and the hewer took his docket without a word, and the stone went over the screens on the Company's time, at the Company's cost. The rail stayed crooked until a crooked rail's consequences worked their way, in the proper order, through the proper hands. Nothing was broken. Nothing was refused. The men had merely stopped protecting the Company from its own arrangements, and stood by, correct and courteous, while it met them.

The returns sagged. Slowly, shallowly — a few points of yield, a creep in the costs, a schedule that ran a quarter-hour behind and then a half — nothing any single report could catch hold of, because no single thing was wrong. Mr. Fenn walked the yard with the look of a man listening for a leak he could hear but not find. Inquiries were held. The inquiries found — Marek could have written the finding before the first question was asked, and savored it privately when it came — that all hands were performing their duties. Which was true. It was the finding itself, exact and entire: all hands were performing their duties, precisely their duties, at last, and nothing more; and the difference between that pit and the pit of a year ago was the whole unpriced gift the Company had been living on without knowing it lived on anything.

It could not be bought back, either. Fenn tried, in the spring — a dinner for the men at the Company's charge, and a notice on the board whose language nobody in the valley had ever seen the Company use before: mindful of the hardships lately borne… the welfare of its people close at heart… Marek read it aloud at the bank in his flat translating voice to a yard that heard it exactly as it was: the machine, having noticed the withdrawal, attempting to purchase with vocabulary what it had previously received free. The words were priced accordingly. The dinner was eaten — the valley wasted nothing — and changed nothing. What had been withdrawn had never been for sale. That was the property of a gift the Company's whole science could not compass: coin could not command it, because it had never been paid for in the first place; and punishment could not restore it, because its absence broke no rule. Surveillance found no ringleader, there being none. You cannot summon weather before the magistrate.

Restraint, Marek wrote in the flyleaf that spring, under the other sentences, had been the Company's real capital all along — not the shafts, not the engines: the daily unrequired generosity of the ruled — and the failing of it made no noise and kept no date. It failed the way a loan fails when the lender, without one word, declines to extend it again.

As for Marek, he had opened a second book.

It was a small book, marbled boards, bought with coin in the market town, and it lived not in the cabin but under the third floorboard, atop the almanac with the unsent letter, and into it, every night, from memory — and his memory for figures was the one endowment no one had ever had to teach him — went the day's true weighing. Tub by tub, the beam's figure and the true figure beside it, the shaving reckoned and carried forward, hewer by hewer, week upon week: the whole quiet arithmetic of the screw, at last, in somebody's ledger. He was two winters gathering it. He never spoke of it. It was evidence for a court that did not exist yet, kept in the faith — it surprised him, finding this faith in himself, patient as the dust — that no arrangement lasts forever, and that when this one broke, in five years or fifty, the breaking would need what breakings always need and never have: the figures, fair-copied, from inside.

And at the beam itself, within the rules, in the yard's own grammar, the beam's mouth began to speak.

"She reads four and three-quarter," Marek would say, entering it — and then, setting the docket in the hewer's hand, in the tone of a man passing the time of day: "Call it a level five, by my eye. She's read light since before my articles."

That was all. Never more. The figure in the book was the office's; the sentence in the air was the yard's; no rule anywhere was broken, for no rule had foreseen the need to forbid a clerk his eye. The men took their dockets and their true count with them down into the dark, and said nothing, and knew — the whole pit knew by Whitsun, and not one word of it ever crossed the yard where a report could pick it up — the men knew their weights now, each his own, week upon week; and the farthings began again in the alehouse, quietly, a subscription with no stated object this time, no checkweighman proposed, no petition drawn: a fund, merely; growing; against nothing that could be named; and the naming of nothing was what made it, for the first time in the valley's history, a thing the Company could not answer.

Josef died in April, in his chair, in the afternoon, with the draughts board set up and his grandson gone to fetch the kettle. The dust closed its account quietly, as it kept it. The row buried him with the honors the row could give — every blind in Prosper Row drawn, every man of the Vesta at the graveside who was not underground, and Brant, too, at the edge of the crowd, come in from the fields; whom Marek, in front of everyone, walked over to and shook by the hand.

The Company sent its regret, and the month's rent notice in the same post, the account being, as always, regular.

Dess kept Col's lamp on her shelf, polished, unlit, beside her nothing else. She paid what the letters said, to the day, exact as frost, and asked the office for nothing again, ever, and gave it nothing again, ever — no petition, no thanks, no posture; she crossed the yard past Fenn himself as past an empty doorway. The Company had heard her once. The silence she paid it back in had a temperature, and even the office's clerks, who could parse nothing that was not raised, were known to find their business at the far counter when she came in.

And Tomas turned twelve, and the tin — refilling, slow as ever, coppers over the buried letter as over a foundation stone — the tin stood at two pounds eleven, which was short, which was always short, and Marek did the sum at the year's turn as he did every year: two years to the premium, twenty months of nothing happening, in a house where he now knew to the grain what nothing cost. The sum came out doubtful. The sum had always come out doubtful.

He set the floorboard back, and went up to where the boy was at his letters by the fire — a real book now, the schoolmaster's own Cobbett, the boy's lips moving with the grammar — and stood in the doorway unseen a moment, in the smell of tallow, listening to his father's cough not come through the wall, and made the only entry that the whole of this account has been keeping toward, and made it silently, in the ledger no office ever reads:

that the sum came out doubtful; and that they would attempt it anyway; and that whether or not one boy's premium was gathered in time, in this valley, in this age of the world — the arrangement now had an end. He could not date it. It would not be in his working life, likely, nor be anything a man could point to when it came, no bell, no banner — only some ordinary Monday, sometime, when the last man extended it the last inch of credit, and the last inch was declined.

But it had an end now, and everyone knew it, and everyone knew that everyone knew — that was the whole difference, and it was total. The pit would turn tomorrow. The rent would be held. The store would open its book. The cage would drop at six, and Marek would climb his two steps, and sharpen his father's knife to his own pencil, and keep on choosing, because there was nothing else on the table.

Nothing had ended.

But nothing felt inevitable anymore.
