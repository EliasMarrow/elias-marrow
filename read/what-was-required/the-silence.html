Chapter Six — The Silence

The timber came through Marek's books too. Everything came through Marek's books. That is the fact this chapter turns on, so let it be set down plainly at the start, the way he never afterward permitted himself to blur it.

Among the cabin's duties was the stores ledger: props and sleepers, oil and cordage, powder and brattice-cloth, everything the pit consumed besides men, entered as it came in by the wagon and issued as it went down by the cage. In the spring of that year — the same spring the petition's answer was still settling over the yard like the last of the sleet — a letter from the office adjusted the allowances. Marek entered the adjustment himself. Timber to the east headings: reduced by a fifth. The east headings were the wet workings, the low seam, the grey band's home, where the roof was known friable and the props stood closest — had always stood closest, close as a fence, because the old viewer had ordered them close, and the old viewer was three years dead and his order, it now appeared, had been all that held the spacing, and the spacing was now found capable of adjustment upon grounds of economy.

Marek read the letter twice, entered it fair, and filed it on the spike.

He understood what it was. Let no one coming after say he did not understand what it was, for he never once said it of himself. A fifth off the timber where the roof was worst was not a figure. It was a distance — the new space between one prop and the next — and into that space, some day of some month of some year, a certain weight of roof was going to descend at a speed no man outruns lying on his side in thirty inches of dark. He was a clerk; distances and weights were his whole learning; he could not not know it. The knowledge arrived in the ordinary post, in a fair round office hand, and he spiked it, and the spike stood on the corner of his desk in the window's light, and for four months he worked beside it the way a man works beside a covered well.

Why did the men not refuse to hew under the wider spacing? They noticed it. Of course they noticed it; their lives were roofs; they read a roof the way Marek read a column. Aldany's crew and the others grumbled at the spacing in the yard grammar — to the props, not to the overseer — timber's grown shy this spring — and the deputies who walked the headings each morning before the shift wrote their daybook reports in the deputies' own dialect, which had been taught the same lesson every other dialect in the valley had been taught. A deputy who wrote the east roof is working and wants timber was a deputy making an accusation against the allowance, and the allowance came from the office. What the daybook said, morning after morning, in the hand of men who ducked under that roof and heard it, was: east headings inspected and found in order, save the roof working somewhat at the third pillar, which is watched.

Which is watched. That was the whole of a man's fear, dressed to pass the office. And it passed. Everything, that spring, was in order, and watched.

And Marek — Marek had his sentence ready. It had been ready since the night of the onions, and it had grown a second clause now: try the beam against the men's own weights; and set the east timber back to the old spacing. One letter. He was the one man in the valley who held both figures — what the beam shaved, what the timber spared — the one man placed to add them into their true single sum, which was: the Company is drawing its dividend out of the margin that keeps these men alive, a whisker at a time, from the wage and from the roof both. He did the addition at his desk in the lamplight and it stood on the page in his own fair hand and he burned the page in the cabin stove, because a page in his hand was his hand, and —

— and here must be set down honestly what he told himself, all four months, because it is what everyone tells themselves, in every valley, in every age, and the telling is the mechanism:

He told himself the moment was wrong. The petition's wound was too fresh; a clerk crying doom now would be heard as the yard's spite in office clothes. He told himself he would speak when the sign came — when the roof gave some warning no daybook could soften, some plain unarguable thing that all men would see together, whereupon his letter would not be an accusation but a confirmation, and would carry. He was waiting, he told himself, for the hour when speaking would do good, and he did not examine — because the not-examining was the mechanism's other half — the fact that an hour when all men see together is an hour that has no need of a voice; that the whole worth of a voice lies in the hours before that hour, when the thing can still be denied, and speaking is dear; that a warning which waits until it is safe has waited, by definition, until it is useless. The right moment he was waiting for did not exist. It has never existed. It is the conscience's unicorn, and men have hunted it to the ends of their lives, and the hunting is called patience, and it is called prudence, and it is called — he wrote the word himself, in the flyleaf, in the fourth month — professional.

And each month of the silence set the price of ending it higher, by exactly the mechanism he was too good a clerk not to see: to speak in June was to be asked why not in May; to speak at midsummer was to account for the whole spring; his silence was compounding, upon the usual terms, until the balance stood where speech itself would indict the speaker — you knew? and wrote it fair? and spiked it? — and a man does not readily testify against his own archive.

So the summer ran down in order, watched.

It came on the fourth of September, at ten minutes past two in the afternoon, and Marek was at the beam.

What he felt first was in the soles of his boots — not a sound, a fact, a single blunt fact delivered upward through two hundred fathoms of rock and the cabin's two wooden steps, as if the earth had shifted its grip on everything standing on it. The ink jarred in the well. The needle of the beam swung once, empty.

Then the sound came, late, the way thunder comes late, but wrong — not from the sky, from under the yard, a long swallowed boom with no edges to it — and then came the third thing, which no man who has stood in a pit yard ever afterward forgets, and which no man can be told: the silence of the machinery. The winding engine stopped. The screens stopped. The rattle that lived under the town like a floor was gone, all at once, everywhere, and in the new enormous quiet the yard stood for one heartbeat entirely still, every face turned to the headgear, and then the yard began to run.

Marek was out of the cabin without his coat. The banksman was already at the shaft with his hands on the signal rope, sending, waiting, sending. From the row above, before any bell was rung, the women were already coming down the hill — that was the thing that broke the heart before any list was posted, how fast the women came, shawls half-pinned, some running with bread still in their hands from the kneading; the valley had done this before; the knowledge of how to do it lived in the walls.

The fall was in the east headings, at the third pillar. It had come down the length of two crews' working, sixty yards of roof, in the space — the men who dug toward them said it after, quietly, and not before the inquest — in the space between one prop and the next.

They dug four days. The valley dug; hewers off shift refused to leave the bank; men came over the moor from two pits east, unasked, with their own tools. On the second day they brought out three men alive from beyond the fall, walled in a rise of good air, and the yard wept and cheered them into the daylight. On the fourth day they brought out the rest.

Five names went up on the board by the bank, in the office's fair round hand, with the Company's regret above them and the funeral fund's provisions below.

The fourth name on the board was Col Dessin, putter, aged fifteen years.

The inquest sat six days after, in the long room of the alehouse, because the alehouse had the only room in the town not owned outright by the Company, though the Company's coal warmed it.

The coroner came out from the market town, a careful, courteous man. The jury was empanelled from the district: two farmers, a wheelwright, shopmen, a maltster — no colliers; colliers were parties, it was explained, and could not sit. The Company was represented. Mr. Fenn gave evidence, grave and exact. The deputies' daybook was read into the record, morning by morning, inspected and found in order… working somewhat… which is watched — and read out in the coroner's level voice the entries sounded, precisely as they had been written to sound, like vigilance. Like men watching faithfully over a roof that then did what no watching can forestall. The surviving deputy wept in the giving of his evidence. Nobody doubted him. That was the deepest gear of the machine, Marek thought, watching from the third bench: it had no need of liars. Every man in that room told the truth as his station had taught him to write it, and the sum of the stations was the lie.

Marek was called. He had known he would be; the stores ledger was evidence of the pit's good conduct of its business.

He took the oath. The coroner's questions came, courteous and narrow: Was the witness the keeper of the stores ledger? He was. Were the issues of timber to the workings duly entered? They were. Did the ledger show timber issued to the east headings in the months preceding the fourth of September? It did. Was timber ever refused that was requisitioned? Never, said Marek — no requisition was ever refused.

All true. The questions walked the path the record made for them, and the record had been built, entry by entry, in his own fair hand, to make exactly this path, and no question came — why should it come? from whom? — asking what the allowance had been, or what it had been the year before, or in whose letter, upon what grounds, the difference lay. The letter was on the spike, in the cabin, two hundred yards from the alehouse door. The addition he had burned was in his head, where it has weight but no jurisdiction. He stood in the box with both, and the coroner said, "Thank you, that is all, unless you wish to add anything," and there it was — unless you wish to add anything — the door, open, in the wall of the world, for the length of one breath.

The room was warm and very quiet. Dess sat by the window in her blacks, her hands still in her lap, in the posture the valley's women had worn at inquests for a hundred years. Fenn's pen had stopped. Somewhere at the back Aldany watched him — Aldany, who had said we did it in the right order, any road, and had not meant it as a knife and it had gone in as one.

"Nothing to add," Marek said. "The books are as I have said."

The verdict came before evening: accidental death, upon the fall of the roof, from natural causes operating in the ordinary hazards of mining; and the jury desired to record that no blame attached to the management, the workings having been regularly inspected; and that no concerns had been raised.

No concerns had been raised. Marek walked home in the dark with the sentence, and it walked well, it kept his pace exactly, and he understood that he would have its company now for the term of his natural life. It did not say no concerns had existed. It was scrupulous, as the machine was always scrupulous. It said none were raised — raised, as a thing is raised from a pit, hauled up into the common light — and it was true, and he was its proof, and the file was closed on it, and forty years on, when every soul in that warm room was dust, some clerk in some office would take the file down and find the surface smooth, and no instrument in the world would show him where, under the fair round hand, five men and the truth lay in the same seam.

Two letters ended the month.

The first was the Company's, to Dess, upon the account of the late Havel Dessin: the wages of Col Dessin, putter, being no longer received, the arrangement of the previous year was at an end, and the account would resume upon the usual terms. The office's punctuality had never rested; the letter was dated within the fortnight of the fall. Marek read it to her at the Sunday table, the whole of it, because his rule was the whole of a letter, and that Sunday he hated his rule as he had never hated the Company.

Dess sat with her hands flat on the table. Then she said: "They heard me once. They'll not hear me again," and folded the letter with the others, and Marek did not understand until years after — until the events of the last chapter of this book — everything that sentence had decided in her, and in how many kitchens that same autumn some cousin of that sentence was being said.

The second letter was Marek's own, and it was never sent, and its history is short. He wrote it entire, that same Sunday night, at the table where the row's letters were written: the beam, the screw, the varnish; the allowance, the spacing, the letter on the spike; the addition, made fair, in his best hand, with nothing softened and every agent named, each act pinned to the hand that did it, in sentences with no exits cut in them, the first first-draft sentences of his life. He signed it. He sat a long while. And this once he did not burn it. He folded it into the flyleafed almanac, under the sentences he kept there, and put the almanac at the bottom of the tin, beneath the one pound four, and set the floorboard back.

A private no, kept in a drawer no inspector would open. It changed nothing. He knew to the farthing how much nothing it changed: harm arrives at its addresses in the same quantity whether the silence around it grieves or approves; a process cannot read the anguish in an unraised hand; to the machine his agony and his assent had weighed alike, all summer, to the last scruple — and the difference between them, which was real, which was the realest thing he owned, had been paid out to no one, ever, but himself. A tax the silent pay inward, at the usual terms.

The pit resumed the Monday after the verdict. The east headings were re-timbered — to the old spacing; the allowance letter came through with the others and Marek entered it fair; economy, it had been found, must yield in some degree to the lessons of experience — and so the props now stood close again over workings where the five had been taken out, and the machine had corrected, at last, everything about the fourth of September except what it was.

The rattle came back under the town like a floor relaid.

And the system, having heard nothing, continued.
